Mission 01 · Warm-up

What Are You Working On?

Start with the designer's real website project. No assumed tech stack or project details.

Follow up: “Can you walk me through it?” · “What changed recently?” · “What is the biggest challenge?” · “Why did you choose that solution?”
Mission 02 · Vocabulary

The Website Change Toolkit

Learn the language used in real design reviews, bug reports, and client requests.

Speaking: Explain two words using an example from a website you've worked on.
Mission 03 · Language Focus

Clarify, Suggest & Push Back

Practice polite requests, explaining consequences, and reporting changes.

Useful professional language

Clarify: “Could you clarify what needs to change?”
Suggest: “I'd recommend moving the CTA higher.”
Explain risk: “If we remove this label, users may get confused.”
Report progress: “I've updated the prototype, but I haven't tested mobile yet.”
Push back: “We can do that, but it may affect the release date.”

Rewrite vague feedback

1. “Make the website better.”
2. “The button is wrong.”
3. “Do it today.”

Mission 05 · Branching Conversation

The Stakeholder Meeting

Choose how to respond to clients, developers, managers, and QA. Every choice has a consequence.

After each response, improvise one follow-up sentence as if you were in the meeting.
Mission 06 · Communicative Practice

The Design Review Simulation

Negotiate scope and priorities in a realistic team meeting.

Role-play · Teacher = Product Owner · Student = UX/UI Designer

Situation: Launch is in three days. Marketing wants a new banner, QA has found a mobile menu bug, and the checkout flow has unclear error messages.

Student's goal: Ask for details, prioritize issues, explain risks, and propose a realistic release plan.

Teacher's challenge: Insist that the banner is urgent. Ask whether everything can be finished tomorrow. Introduce a surprise: the new design fails a keyboard test.

The UX/UI Decision Room

Teacher guide · Timing & differentiation

Suggested 75–95 minutes or two shorter lessons. Warm-up 8 min, vocabulary 10, language 12, tickets 20, branching 10, simulation 15, proposal 12, final 8. For B1, allow preparation and sentence frames; for stronger students, require trade-offs, specific acceptance criteria, and stakeholder negotiation. The sample website is fictional; ask the student to compare it with their real work without assuming their stack, product, or company.

Teacher can navigate freely and skip activities without earning XP. Completion is teacher/student-controlled and does not imply automatically verified performance. No audio.
