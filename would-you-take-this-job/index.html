Would You Take This Job?

Compare five careers, imagine how each would change your week, and defend the life you’d choose.

B2YOUNG ADULTS60–75 MIN8 MISSIONSSPEAKING-FIRST
Mission 01 · Priorities

What Makes a Career Worth It?

Compare your priorities with a partner before seeing the five careers.

Ask your partner for a specific example that shows why their top priority matters.

Rank pay, predictable hours, free weekends, meaningful work, and variety. Share your top two and your deal-breaker.

Ask your partner: “What would make you change your mind?” Then explain one priority you have in common and one you weigh differently.

Mission 03 · Language

Compare, Qualify, Push Back

Use these expressions to compare careers, describe hypothetical routines, and disagree politely.

Reveal the expressions and adapt the examples to your own life.

Weigh a trade-off · weigh up, be worth it

“We’d need to weigh up the higher pay against the nights away.”

“The extra R$2,200 would be worth it if I still had enough time at home.”

Imagine the routine · If I were…, I’d…

“If I were a police officer on this rotation, I’d have to plan around night shifts.”

“Working alternating weekends would make it harder to see my friends.”

Concede and challenge · I see your point, but…

“I see your point about flexibility, but how much would evening support disrupt your plans?”

“I’d agree, provided that I could keep one weekend free each month.”

Choose two careers. Compare one detail, imagine one consequence, and respond to a partner’s objection.
Mission 04 · Life swap

A Week in Your New Life

Imagine doing one of these jobs for six months. Walk your partner through a workday and a day off.

Think about when you’d have time for the people and activities that matter to you.

Mission 05 · Debate

Defend It, Then Switch Sides

Pick a motion. Give a 45-second argument, answer one objection, then make the strongest case for the other side.

When you switch sides, respond to the strongest point your partner made.

  • A predictable schedule is worth more than an extra R$4,000 a month. Compare the teacher and developer.
  • The doctor’s higher pay makes up for the rotating nights. Compare the doctor and developer.
  • Variety at work is worth giving up predictable free time. Compare the flight attendant and police officer.

Before you challenge your partner, summarize their point in one sentence. Use a schedule or pay detail to support your response.

Mission 06 · Twist

Would One Change Change Your Mind?

Choose a career you would turn down. Reveal a change and decide whether it would move that career up your list.

Explain why the change would or wouldn’t make you reconsider.

On your first turn, keep the original pay and schedule unless the card changes them. Then ask your partner what it would take to make them reconsider.

Mission 07 · Decision

Make Your Final Ranking

Rank all five careers for yourself. Then give a 90-second verdict on your top choice and the one you’d turn down first.

Give one concrete reason for each of your first and last choices.

Use at least three details across pay, schedule, and lifestyle. Your partner challenges one assumption. Reply, then explain what would make you reconsider.

Mission 08 · Reflection

What Changed Your Mind?

Tell your partner which argument surprised you most. Then give each other one piece of feedback.

Tell your partner which part of their argument was most convincing and why.

  • Did you ask a follow-up question that got a specific answer?
  • Did you connect a job detail to a realistic change in someone’s life?
  • Did you acknowledge a counterargument before responding?
