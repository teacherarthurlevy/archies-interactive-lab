MISSION 01 · EXAM REVIEW

Present Simple → Past Simple

I like → she said she liked

Quick grammar reminder

When the reporting verb is in the past, we often move Present Simple back to Past Simple.

Direct: “I like your new car.” → Reported: He said (that) he liked my new car.

Remember: Pronouns and possessives may change depending on who is speaking.

MISSION 02 · EXAM REVIEW

Present Continuous → Past Continuous

I am getting → she was getting

Quick grammar reminder

Present Continuous (am/is/are + -ing) usually shifts to Past Continuous (was/were + -ing).

Direct: “I am getting married.” → She said (that) she was getting married.

Remember: Keep the -ing form; change the auxiliary verb and pronouns.

MISSION 03 · EXAM REVIEW

Present Perfect & Past Perfect

have bought → had bought

Quick grammar reminder

Present Perfect (have/has + past participle) usually becomes Past Perfect (had + past participle). The worksheet also shows Past Simple → Past Perfect.

Direct: “We have bought the tickets.” → He said they had bought the tickets. Direct: “I missed the train.” → He said he had missed the train.

Remember: Extra extension: Present Perfect Continuous (have been + -ing) → Past Perfect Continuous (had been + -ing). This extension is not shown on the photographed worksheet.

MISSION 04 · EXAM REVIEW

Past Simple → Past Perfect

I ate → she had eaten

Quick grammar reminder

In a past-reporting context, Past Simple often moves back to Past Perfect (had + past participle).

Direct: “I ate too much.” → She said she had eaten too much.

Remember: Irregular past participles: ate → eaten, went → gone, saw → seen, took → taken.

MISSION 05 · EXAM REVIEW

Future: Will & Going To

will → would · am going to → was going to

Quick grammar reminder

Will usually changes to would. Am/is/are going to changes to was/were going to.

Direct: “I will see you later.” → He said he would see me later. “I am going to join the class.” → He said he was going to join the class.

Remember: Watch both the future structure and the pronouns.

MISSION 06 · EXAM REVIEW

Say vs Tell

said (that) · told someone (that)

Quick grammar reminder

Say focuses on the message: said (that) + clause. Tell normally needs a listener: told + someone + (that) + clause.

She said (that) she was tired. / She told me (that) she was tired.

Remember: Not 'she told that...' and not 'she said me...'. Note: 'say to someone' is also possible.

MISSION 07 · EXAM REVIEW

Time & Word Changes

now → then · today → that day

Quick grammar reminder

Reported speech may change time/place words if the reporting context has changed.

now → then · today → that day · tomorrow → the next day · yesterday → the day before · here → there · this → that

Remember: These changes are not automatic: if the time or place is still the same, the original word may remain.
