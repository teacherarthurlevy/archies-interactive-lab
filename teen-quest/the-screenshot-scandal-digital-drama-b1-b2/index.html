Who said what? What really happened? You decide.

01 · WARM-UP & ENGAGEMENT

Before You Share…

Discuss these questions with your teacher or partner. Give reasons and examples.

1. Have you ever seen a rumor spread in a group chat?
2. Can a screenshot tell the whole story?
3. What would you do if a friend shared something private?
4. Is deleting a message the same as fixing a mistake?
SPEAK · 5–7 MIN

Mini decision: Someone sends you an embarrassing screenshot. Do you share it, ignore it, or ask for context? Explain.

02 · VOCABULARY DISCOVERY

Six Words, Big Consequences

Reveal each word, use it in a sentence, then move on. Skipping never awards XP.

Speak it: Choose three words and explain a real or imaginary situation using them.

03 · LANGUAGE FOCUS

Report It. Don't Distort It.

Reported speech

Direct: “I didn't post it.”
Reported: She said (that) she hadn't posted it.

Direct: “I will explain.”
Reported: He said he would explain.

Tell + person: She told me she was upset.

Modals of deduction

must = strong conclusion
might / could = possibility
can't = strong belief something is impossible

“The timestamp is different. It might be an old screenshot.”

04 · MAIN INTERACTIVE CHALLENGE

The Evidence Room 🔎

Monday morning: a screenshot appears in the class chat. It seems to show Maya saying, “Leo is so annoying. Don't invite him.” Leo is hurt. Maya says the screenshot is missing context.

Evidence A · Screenshot

The image shows Maya's name, but the message above it is cropped out.

Evidence B · Time

The screenshot was taken two weeks ago, not yesterday.

Evidence C · Maya

“I was quoting someone else's comment. I didn't say that about Leo.”

Evidence D · Leo

“I don't know what to believe. Everyone has seen it.”

Investigate

What do we know for sure? What is only a possibility? What evidence is missing?

Speaking challenge: Give a 45-second update: “According to the screenshot… Maya said that… However, it might… We should…”
05 · BRANCHING CONVERSATION

You're in the Group Chat

Your friend says: “Everyone's sharing this screenshot. Should I repost it?”

06 · COMMUNICATIVE PRACTICE

The Friendship Repair Lab

Role-play A · The mediator

Student A: Maya. Student B: Leo. A third student mediates. Clarify what was said and propose a fair next step.

Role-play B · The journalist

Interview two classmates about the rumor. Then report what they said without adding unsupported claims.

Debate · 60 seconds

“Schools should punish students who share private screenshots.” Agree or disagree?

Pressure test

Your best friend insists the screenshot is real. Disagree respectfully using might, could, and must.

Teacher follow-ups: What makes evidence reliable? Who is responsible for the damage? What would rebuild trust?

07 · CREATE & APPLY

Write the Message That Changes Everything

Write a 70–100-word message for the class chat. Your goal: stop speculation, protect privacy, and suggest a constructive next step.

Include: one reported statement, two modals of deduction, and one clear request.

Useful sentence starters

“Maya said that…” · “The screenshot might…” · “We can't know for sure…” · “Could we please…”

08 · FINAL QUEST & REFLECTION

The Truth, the Rumor & Your Decision

Final speaking quest (90 seconds): Summarize the case, distinguish facts from assumptions, report what two people said, and explain your decision.

Use: said / told + might / must / can't + because / however.
