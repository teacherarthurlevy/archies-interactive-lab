03 · Notice & Practice

Language Focus: Skeptical English

Expressing doubt

“I'm not entirely convinced.”
“Something doesn't add up.”
“That sounds a bit far-fetched.”

Showing belief

“I buy it.”
“That checks out.”
“I'm inclined to believe you.”

Interrogating politely

“Could you walk us through what happened?”
“What happened next?”
“Can you clarify the timeline?”

Reported speech

Direct: “I fell into a tank.”
Reported: She claimed she had fallen into a tank.

Grammar strategy: Use past simple to narrate events; past perfect to refer to an earlier past event; modals of deduction for evidence: “He might be exaggerating.” “She can't have signed it herself.”

    06 · Speak & Negotiate

    Communicative Practice: Jury Room

    Work in pairs or small groups. One person defends a confession, one interrogates, and one decides.

    🎭 The storyteller

    Explain when, where, who, why and what happened next. Maintain a consistent timeline.

    🔎 The investigator

    Ask at least five follow-up questions. Challenge two details respectfully.

    ⚖️ The juror

    Summarize the evidence, identify contradictions and explain your verdict.

    🗣️ The moderator

    Invite quieter speakers, manage turn-taking and seek a unanimous decision.

    Useful phrases: “I'm leaning toward…” · “That doesn't quite add up.” · “What evidence supports that?” · “Can we agree on a verdict?”

    Discussion challenge

    Is it fair to judge whether someone is lying based on their nervousness? Explain why or why not.

    07 · Create & Apply

    Create: Your Confession

    Write one true story or a believable fictional story. Don't reveal which it is. Your partner must interrogate you.

    Speak for 60–90 seconds. Your partner asks five questions. Answer without reading your text.
